# 📊 Analysis

This section details the various statistical analyses performed on student engagement data, focusing on descriptive statistics, skewness and kurtosis, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing.

## 📝 Task 1: Descriptive Statistics
Descriptive statistics provide an overview of student engagement in terms of minutes watched. Key metrics include:

- **Mean**: Average minutes watched by students in Q4 2021 and Q4 2022.
- **Median**: Middle value to assess typical student behavior.
- **Standard Deviation**: Measures data dispersion.

### Key Insights:
- **Paid-plan students** showed a significant increase in engagement from Q4 2021 to Q4 2022.
- **Free-plan students** exhibited an increase in mean minutes watched, but the median decreased, indicating higher variability.

## 📊 Task 2: Skewness and Kurtosis
Skewness and kurtosis help analyze the shape of the engagement distribution.

- **Skewness**: Measures asymmetry in the data distribution.
- **Kurtosis**: Indicates whether the distribution is heavy-tailed (leptokurtic) or light-tailed (platykurtic).

### Observations:
- **Paid-plan students**: Skewness increased from **0.63 (Q4 2021) to 7.07 (Q4 2022)**, indicating a right-skewed distribution.
- **Free-plan students**: Skewness increased from **1.17 to 15.06**, suggesting a few students watched significantly more content.

## 🎯 Task 3: Confidence Intervals
Confidence intervals estimate the true mean engagement of students with a degree of certainty.

- **Paid-plan students**: The confidence interval for the mean increased, confirming higher engagement in Q4 2022.
- **Free-plan students**: The confidence interval decreased, highlighting lower engagement levels for the majority of students.

## 🔬 Task 4: Hypothesis Testing
Two-sample F-tests and t-tests were conducted to compare engagement between paid and free-plan students.

- **Variance Test (F-test)**: Rejected the null hypothesis of equal variances (p-value = 0).
- **Mean Comparison (t-test)**: A left-tailed t-test confirmed that engagement among paid-plan students in Q4 2022 was significantly higher than free-plan students.

## 📌 Summary of Findings
- Paid-plan students showed a **substantial** increase in engagement.
- Free-plan students displayed **high variability**, with a few students watching significantly more content.
- Statistical tests confirmed that engagement differences between the two groups were **statistically significant**.

This analysis highlights the impact of subscription plans on student engagement and suggests targeted interventions for free-plan students to enhance retention and participation.
