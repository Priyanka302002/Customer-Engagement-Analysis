# 📌 Conclusion

The analysis of student engagement based on subscription plans provides valuable insights into user behavior and learning patterns. Key takeaways from the study include:

- **Paid-plan students** showed a significant increase in engagement from Q4 2021 to Q4 2022, as seen in higher mean and median minutes watched.
- **Free-plan students** displayed more variability in engagement, with an increase in average minutes watched but a decrease in the median, indicating inconsistent learning behavior.
- **Skewness and kurtosis** results highlighted that both groups had right-skewed distributions, suggesting that a small number of students watched significantly more content.
- **Confidence interval analysis** confirmed that paid-plan students consistently had higher engagement levels.
- **Hypothesis testing** validated the statistical significance of engagement differences, reinforcing the impact of subscription plans on student behavior.

### 🔍 Final Thoughts

The findings emphasize the importance of structured learning plans in increasing student engagement. While paid plans promote steady growth in engagement, free-plan users may benefit from targeted strategies such as personalized recommendations, gamification, or limited-time premium access to enhance their learning experience.

Future studies could explore additional factors affecting engagement, such as course difficulty, content diversity, and student demographics, to further refine engagement strategies.
