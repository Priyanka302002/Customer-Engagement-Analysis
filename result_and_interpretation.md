# 📊 Results and Interpretation  

This section presents the key findings from the analysis of student engagement based on subscription plans.  

## 🔹 Descriptive Statistics  

- **Paid-plan students:** Showed a significant increase in engagement from Q4 2021 to Q4 2022, with both the mean and median minutes watched increasing.  
- **Free-plan students:** Although the average minutes watched increased, the median decreased, indicating inconsistent engagement patterns.  

## 🔹 Skewness and Kurtosis  

- **Paid-plan students:** Skewness increased from 0.63 (Q4 2021) to 7.07 (Q4 2022), showing a more right-skewed distribution.  
- **Free-plan students:** Skewness increased significantly from 1.17 to 15.06, suggesting a small number of students watched significantly more content.  

## 🔹 Confidence Intervals  

- **Paid-plan students:** The confidence interval for the mean minutes watched widened, confirming a strong engagement increase.  
- **Free-plan students:** The confidence interval narrowed, indicating less consistent engagement growth.  

## 🔹 Hypothesis Testing  

- **Variance Comparison (F-Test):** The null hypothesis of equal variances was rejected, showing that engagement variability differed between the two groups.  
- **Mean Comparison (T-Test):** A left-tailed test confirmed that paid-plan students had significantly higher engagement in Q4 2022 than free-plan students.  

## 📌 Key Insights  

- Subscription plans play a crucial role in student engagement, with **paid users showing more consistent growth**.  
- **Free-plan users exhibit varied engagement**, suggesting a need for intervention strategies such as personalized content recommendations or engagement incentives.  
- The significant skewness increase indicates that **a small subset of users engages heavily**, while many remain inactive.  

These insights can guide data-driven strategies to improve engagement and retention among students.
